First answer for Kaarigar Home

Your ad spend doubled but orders barely moved. Here is what the signals say.

This answer has seven layers. Each one adds a dimension that a generic LLM response would skip. Scroll through them.

1 · Position
The constraint is more likely offer-audience fit than ad mechanics (targeting)
Spend rose across the same audiences with the same offer, and the drop is in conversion (people who see it but do not buy), not reach (people who see it at all). This pattern points at the message, not the media. SOLID
2 · Why
Doubling spend into unchanged targeting usually moves impressions before it moves orders
Your symptom pattern matches: impressions up, CTR flat, conversion down. The audience is seeing more of the same thing and responding less. That is a message fatigue signal, not a targeting failure. (CTR = click-through rate, the % of people who click after seeing)
3 · Source and evidence state
Founder-stated figures, unverified
Evidence state: provisional PROVISIONAL. You shared the numbers but we have not seen the raw data yet. No metric definitions supplied. "Orders" could mean different things depending on how your system counts them.
4 · Assumed / unknown
Two things we assumed and one we cannot see
Assumed: your tracking did not change mid-quarter. Assumed: "doubled" means roughly 2x, not a budget reallocation across channels. Unknown: your conversion definition and denominator (denominator = what you are dividing by to calculate the rate). FOG
5 · The smallest decisive question
Before recommending a cut, one question changes everything
Is "orders" here first-time customers, or all orders including repeats? If repeats are holding steady and new customer orders dropped, the problem is acquisition messaging. If both dropped, the problem is broader.
6 · Proposed first move
Do not cut spend yet. Pull one report first.
Split orders by new vs. returning for the two quarters. That single split changes which lever matters. If new customer orders dropped while repeat held, your ad creative is the problem. If both dropped, your product page or pricing drifted. One report, one answer.
